\begin{abstract}
Tau Core asks whether observed four-dimensional physics can be represented as
an observer-indexed, information-losing readout of a richer atemporal
morphological object.  We first define the minimal architecture
\(s\mapsto\rho_\tau(s)\mapsto M_\tau\mapsto\Desc_O(M_\tau)\), together with
its source-freeze, null-quotient, and no-double-counting conditions.  We then
go beyond a framework-only statement by fixing one minimum-viable completion
at the level of a common variational packet.  In that completion a stabilized
body \(M_*\) is
solved before terminal variation, and a single once-counted generating
functional supplies coframe, gauge/current, quantum-effect, and clock rows.
Under stated Lorentzian, regularity, state/effect, and observer-carrier
assumptions, the construction has compatible conditional limits: a
semiclassical Einstein equation with renormalized quantum stress and the
fixed-background Schr\"odinger--Born description.  The same frozen completion
also yields a falsifiable observer-resolution inequality
\[
  \delta t_{\min,O}\geq
  \frac{2\hbar}{\Delta H}\arcsin\frac{\epsilon_O}{2}
  \geq\frac{\hbar\epsilon_O}{\Delta H},
\]
where \(x_A=\epsilon_O^2\) is an independently reconstructed dimensionless
body-action record-separation ratio.  In the adopted common-Gram
minimum-lift completion, Uhlmann minimization proves
\(x_A=x_Q=2(1-\sqrt F)\) and
\(D_B=2\arcsin(\epsilon_O/2)\), so \(\epsilon_O\) lower-bounds the minimum
Fubini--Study/Bures record angle.  The equality \(x_A=x_Q\), reconstructed by
independent action-curvature and state-tomography protocols, is the primary
Tau-specific discriminator.  Neither the inverse-energy dependence nor
the standard chord-to-angle conversion is claimed as new.  The Tau-specific
candidate is instead the source-frozen physical identification of the
morphological Gram chord with the observer carrier's Uhlmann-amplitude
distance.
Relative to the present source axioms, physical record-edge occupation and
this finite body--Uhlmann identity are proved logically independent: neither
selects the other, although a realized test requires both together with an
operational hold condition.
Within the declared source-minimal primary-SHR completion, however, seed-order
reconstruction, pure Hodge-isometric realization, and the induced
Hodge--Dirac operator derive the required spectral block decomposition.  A
separately stated source-owned block connector then makes the finite identity
a conditional theorem; a
nonzero partial-body metric defect requires omitted response rank.  Treating
the morphological body as the exhaustive stabilized base--seed response
absorbs every such occupied block into the complete body.  Universal-seed
order completeness then forces chain descent on the physical quotient, so the
affiliated recovery construction is stable under faithful rich-body completion
and does not require minimum body dimension.  Spectral additivity alone does
not select the finite body--Uhlmann connector.
For finite source algebras, a complete algebra/state/role signature determines
its minimal cyclic realization up to typed unitary equivalence.  This is a
representation-uniqueness result conditional on source ownership, not a
derivation of that signature from the present parent.  Complete sector
marginals do not determine the joint packet: in a four-qubit control, even all
proper marginals are blind to the $81$-dimensional genuine four-body Pauli
subspace.  One source-owned onto joint incidence with positive stiffness is a
sufficient finite replacement.  These results sharpen the physical selection
problem without closing it.
The inequality concerns one stable record, not a universal discrete time atom.
The result is a conditional theory closure: the current base--seed reduct
admits alternative positive completions, and nature-level selection,
fundamental-constant prediction, ultraviolet and Standard-Model completion,
and empirical confirmation remain open.  The manuscript therefore presents
one auditable candidate with standard-limit checks, a hard claim ledger, and a
predeclared discriminator, not a replacement for general relativity or quantum
theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and claim boundary}

This manuscript states the Tau Core foundation language and the strongest
currently frozen physical completion in a form that can be criticized,
reproduced, and rejected.  It is self-contained: no result from another Tau
Core manuscript, repository, or unpublished analysis is used as a premise or
as evidence for any claim made here.

The claim boundary has two levels.  At the foundational level, the paper
defines an atemporal body/readout architecture and its audit discipline.  At
the completion level, it fixes one common functional and proves consequences
conditional on an explicit assumption ledger.  Neither level supplies
empirical validation or a theorem that the physical base--seed source selects
that completion.  The main exclusions are summarized below and itemized again
near the end of the manuscript.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.90\linewidth}
\textbf{Reviewer-facing claim box.}
This paper claims (i) a formal body/readout architecture and failure
discipline, and (ii) one \emph{conditional} minimum-viable completion with
compatible GR/optics and quantum/Born limits plus one frozen
observer-resolution lower-bound discriminator.  It does not claim empirical validation,
uniqueness, nature-level source selection, a new force, dark-matter or
dark-energy replacement, ultraviolet quantum gravity, or full
Standard-Model recovery.  Every conditional result below is void as a claim
about Nature unless the common completion is independently selected or its
predeclared discriminator survives testing.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The central idea can be written in one line:
\begin{equation}
  s \longmapsto \Rmap(s) \longmapsto U_i^{\Mta}(\Rmap(s)).
  \label{eq:core-spine}
\end{equation}
The first arrow is not time evolution.  It is a parent-side response relation.
The second arrow is a body-conditioned readout.  The ``readout'' is not an
extra physical substance or history store: in the completion developed below
it is an observer-specific realization of ordering and compatibility already
resident in the stabilized body.  A physical branch appears only after the
descent has supplied a codomain, a null policy, a closure test, and a
validation boundary.  When \(\Mta\) is fixed by the declared source class, the
superscript is often suppressed and \(U_i\) is written for
\(U_i^{\Mta}\).

In a deterministic branch one may write
\[
  \Rmap:\Ssrc\to\RTspace.
\]
In a relational branch the same symbol denotes either a relation
\[
  \Rmap\subseteq \Ssrc\times\RTspace
\]
or, equivalently, a set-valued response rule
\[
  \Rmap:\Ssrc\to\mathcal P(\RTspace).
\]
Such a branch must declare whether it uses the whole admissible response class
or an endpoint-blind source-freeze rule selecting a representative.

Equivalently, the paper works with the following foundation package:
\[
  \mathfrak T
  =
  (\Ssrc,\RTspace,\Rmap,\Mta,\{U_i\},\{N_i\},\{J_i\}).
\]
Here \(\Ssrc\) is a seed/source domain, \(\RTspace\) is a formal response
domain, \(\Rmap\) is a map, relation, or set-valued response rule, \(\Mta\)
is the morphological body, \(U_i\) are sector readouts, \(N_i\) are null or
quotient policies, and \(J_i\) are optional relifts.  This package is an
organizing object for later branches (Fig.~\ref{fig:core-spine}), not a proof
that Nature uses it.

In informal Tau Core discussions, the phrase \emph{Tau Core base field} refers
to this parent-side response substrate, represented here by
\((\Ssrc,\RTspace,\Rmap)\), together with the morphological body \(\Mta\).
The phrase is heuristic.  In this paper it does not denote a physical field
with an action, equations of motion, units, stress-energy, or empirical
calibration.

\section{Why a foundation paper is needed}

Several themes in modern foundations already question whether time, spacetime,
or local dynamics should be primitive.  Canonical quantum gravity contains the
well-known problem of time, where a timeless constraint equation can coexist
with effective dynamics \cite{WheelerDeWitt1967,Rovelli2004}.  Relational
time constructions show how dynamics can be described through correlations
rather than external evolution \cite{PageWootters1983}.  Timeless or
configuration-space views have also been explored philosophically and
mathematically \cite{Barbour1999}.  The thermal-time hypothesis is especially
close in spirit because it treats time as state-dependent rather than as an
external primitive \cite{ConnesRovelli1994}.  Causal-set and generalized
quantum-mechanics programs provide other examples of frameworks that change
the primitive descriptive layer rather than merely adding a term to an
equation \cite{Bombelli1987,Hartle1991}.  Quantum reconstruction programs show
that large parts of quantum formalism can be organized by operational
principles rather than simply postulated
\cite{Hardy2001,Chiribella2011,MasanesMueller2011}.  Constructor theory is a
useful methodological comparison because it also tries to state physical
content through possible/impossible transformations rather than through one
specific dynamical equation \cite{Deutsch2013,DeutschMarletto2015}.

Tau Core does not claim to solve those programs.  It uses a different
organizing question:
\begin{quote}
Can observed sector descriptions be treated as readouts of a deeper parent response, so
that time evolution, quantum state, gravity, mass, and observer-time are
effective sector descriptions rather than primitive categories?
\end{quote}
This question is intentionally broader than any one branch.  If it is too
broad, it becomes metaphysics.  The purpose of the present paper is to prevent
that drift by specifying the minimal objects that must exist before a Tau Core
statement becomes mathematically meaningful.

The immediate motivation is the following tension.  Many physical descriptions
use variables that are defined only after an observer, a section, a clock, a
measurement basis, or a spacetime background is in place.  But the same
description often treats those variables as if they were primitive.  Tau Core
instead separates:
\[
  \text{parent response}
  \quad \text{from} \quad
  \text{sector readout}.
\]
That separation is the entire point of the core-spine relation
\eqref{eq:core-spine}.

\section{The name Tau Core}

The name ``Tau Core'' is historical.  The program began from a time-based
intuition: perhaps the time seen by a four-dimensional observer is not a
primitive background, but a projected response.  This explains the use of
\(\tau\), a symbol often associated with time or proper time in physics.

In the present architecture, however, \(\tau\) does not mean ordinary time.  It
names the parent-state side of the theory.  Time is one possible readout from
that side.  The distinction is important:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Symbol or phrase & Meaning here & Not claimed here\\
\midrule
\(\tau\) & parent-state side & ordinary time itself\\
\(X_{\tau}\) & parent Tau state & detected physical field\\
\(\Rmap(s)\) & endpoint-blind response & time evolution\\
\(U_{\rm time}\) & observer-indexed clock descent & nature-selected universal clock\\
\(\Mta\) & morphological body & observed 4D shape\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Thus the theory is time-motivated but not time-reductionist.  It does not say
that everything is made of time.  In the completion used here, causal
orientation and ordering belong to the body, while measurable elapsed time and
its observer resolution arise in the observer descent.  The symbol \(\tau\)
names the deeper side; it is not an additional parent clock.

\section{Difference from a block universe}

A block-universe reading treats a four-dimensional spacetime history as the
final object.  The comparison target is not a straw man.  The relativistic
background begins with Minkowski's four-dimensional spacetime formulation
\cite{Minkowski1909}.  The Rietdijk--Putnam--Maxwell line of argument then
uses relativity of simultaneity to motivate four-dimensionalism or eternalism
\cite{Rietdijk1966,Putnam1967,Maxwell1985,Thyssen2023RPM}.  Standard
surveys of the philosophy of spacetime and time describe the resulting
debates around being, becoming, eternalism, and the block universe
\cite{Savitt2021,MarkosianSullivan2025,PetersonSilberstein2010,Thyssen2020}.
In that setting one may summarize the block-universe reading as
\begin{equation}
  \text{Reality} = M_{4D}.
\end{equation}
Tau Core instead treats a four-dimensional block, when such a block is a good
description, as a readout:
\begin{equation}
  M_{4D} = U_{4D}^{\Mta}(\Rmap(s)).
  \label{eq:block-readout}
\end{equation}
This difference has physical content only if additional non-spacetime readout
constraints can be source-frozen beyond the spacetime block itself.  If no
non-spacetime readout imposes such restrictions, the distinction remains a
metaphysical rephrasing rather than a physical branch.
The difference is not cosmetic.  In a block-universe view the four-dimensional
object is the final arena.  In Tau Core it is an arena produced by a readout.
The parent response can in principle have distinctions that one readout
identifies, another ignores, and a third translates into effective
readout-variation.

Tau Core can be compatible with an effective 4D block without taking it as
ontologically final or refuting block-universe accounts.  It instead relocates
the block from final ontology to sector output (Fig.~\ref{fig:block-vs-tau}):
\[
  \text{block as final object}
  \quad \longrightarrow \quad
  \text{block as } U_{4D}\text{-readout}.
\]

\section{Difference from ordinary spacetime field theory}

An ordinary field theory begins with a field on spacetime:
\begin{equation}
  \phi: M_{4D} \to V,
\end{equation}
and then studies the dynamics of \(\phi(x,t)\).  Tau Core rewrites the order:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{\rm obs}(x,t)
  =
  U_{\phi}(\Rmap(s)).
  \label{eq:field-readout}
\end{equation}
The observed field is a readout.  The spacetime coordinates are also part of
the readout structure.  This does not make ordinary field theory wrong.  It
makes it effective: a very successful description after a particular sector
readout has become stable.

This is why Tau Core cannot claim physical success merely by writing a new
readout map.  To become physics, a readout must recover known limits and must
survive empirical controls.  If \(U_{\phi}\) is arbitrary, the framework is
empty.  The research program therefore needs source-frozen readout rules,
quotient-safe null policies, and explicit failure conditions.

The MVP completion does not discard spacetime field theory.  It recovers an
ordinary Lorentzian effective field theory on a selected descended leaf and
uses its standard variational machinery.  The proposed difference lies
upstream: the coframe, state/effect pair, and observer clock are jointly typed
outputs of a frozen body rather than mutually independent primitives.  Whether
that upstream organization is physically real is exactly the selection and
QOR-test question left open here.

\section{Core vocabulary}

\begin{definition}[Parent seed]
A parent seed \(s \in \Ssrc\) is a Tau-side source datum.  It is not an
observed endpoint and must not be chosen by looking at the endpoint residual
that a later readout is supposed to explain.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Endpoint-blind Tau response]
An endpoint-blind Tau response is a map or relation
\[
  \Rmap: \Ssrc \to \RTspace
\]
whose input is the parent seed and admissible parent-side constraints, not the
observational endpoint.  Endpoint blindness is a non-leakage rule, not a proof
that the response is physically real.
When \(\Rmap\) is relational rather than single-valued, \(\Rmap(s)\) denotes
the admissible response class, or a representative chosen by an explicitly
declared source-freeze rule.  No endpoint residual may be used to select the
representative.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Morphological body]
The Tau-side morphological body \(\Mta\) is the intrinsic structured body that
conditions readouts.  It is not an observed 4D morphology label.  It may carry
sector, branch, wall, null, Hessian-support, admissibility, global-order, and
observer/path-relevant structure.  A projected morphology is only a readout
handle on this deeper body.
\end{definition}

For Foundation Paper I the minimal mathematical type is:
\[
  \Mta\in\mathcal M_\tau^{\rm adm},
\]
where \(\mathcal M_\tau^{\rm adm}\) is a declared class of admissible
morphological records.  The required part of such a record is deliberately
small: it must specify the source domain, the readout family, the null policy,
and the admissibility/freeze rule used by the branch.  Sector decompositions,
walls, Hessian support, global order, and observer/path structure are optional
branch data unless the branch explicitly uses them in its readout rule.  A
branch may instantiate such a record schematically as
\[
  \Mta=(S,B,W,N,H,A;\Theta),
\]
where \(S\) records sector decomposition, \(B\) branch/support structure,
\(W\) wall or transition adjacency, \(N\) null/protected structure, \(H\)
Hessian-response support, \(A\) admissibility constraints, and \(\Theta\)
optional interface/order/path data.  Thus \(U_i^{\Mta}\) is not merely a
decorative superscript: it means that the readout map is selected from a typed
family
\[
  U_i:\mathcal M_\tau^{\rm adm}\times\RTspace\to\Obs_i.
\]
This is still a minimal type declaration, not a construction theorem for
\(\mathcal M_\tau^{\rm adm}\).  In particular, an observed galaxy morphology,
a quantum access structure, and an observer-time structure may be different
projected handles on different subrecords of \(\Mta\), not identical kinds of
morphology.

\begin{definition}[Readout]
A readout is a morphology-conditioned sector map.  Equivalently, one may write
\[
  U_i^{\Mta}:\RTspace\to\Obs_i,
  \qquad
  \text{or}
  \qquad
  U_i:\mathcal M_{\tau}\times \RTspace\to\Obs_i.
\]
The codomain \(\Obs_i\) may be a spacetime description, a time ordering, a
quantum access state, a gravitational response, a mass/source record, or a
more specialized observable structure.  Each readout has its own null
directions and validation boundary.  When \(\Mta\) is fixed by the declared
source class, we suppress it and write \(U_i(r)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cross-scale readout composition]
Readout families are branch-distinct but not assumed independent.  A candidate
kernel \(K_i\) for one family is admissible only after the source, carrier,
quotient, and observable dependencies that compositionally link it to neighboring
readout families have been checked, or explicitly frozen as negligible under the
declared assumptions.  Equivalently, Tau Core treats physical readouts as a
compatibility-coupled composition hypergraph, not as isolated fit modules.
\end{definition}

This is an admissibility rule, not a derivation of unification.  It forbids
treating a gravity kernel as independent of the particle, mass, material,
radiation, and path readouts composing its carrier
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cross-scale-readout-composition}).  It also prevents double counting:
two source-overlapping readout terms cannot be added as independent corrections
unless their non-overlap and quotient visibility have been declared.

\begin{definition}[Relift]
A relift for a readout \(U_i\) is a map or partial map
\[
  J_i : \Obs_i \to \RTspace
\]
that attempts to reconstruct a parent-response representative from a readout
record.  It need not exist globally and need not be unique.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Readout obstruction]
Given \(r=\Rmap(s)\), the primary defect object is not a subtraction but an
obstruction record
\[
  \omega_i(r)=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  J_iU_i(r)\,N_i\,r,
  \label{eq:obstruction}
\]
whenever a relift and null relation have been declared.  Thus
\(\omega_i(r)\neq 0\) records that the readout and its allowed relift fail to
recover the parent-response representative modulo the declared null relation.
Only in a chosen affine or linearized chart may this be represented by the
coordinate expression
\[
  h_i(r)=J_i(U_i(r))-r.
  \label{eq:defect}
\]
\end{definition}

The obstruction condition and its chart-level representative are not
dark-matter formulas or quantum-collapse formulas.  They are abstract
incompleteness records.  A physical branch must later specify the codomain,
quotient, units, known limits, and empirical test.

\section{Minimal readout calculus}

The formalism above is intentionally weak.  A readout is not yet a functor, a
field equation, a probability rule, or a variational principle.  Nevertheless,
some minimal calculus is needed if the framework is to avoid being only
language.

The mathematics in this section is standard.  A readout induces a partition of
the parent-response space; quotient safety is well-definedness on the
corresponding quotient; refinement is the ordinary refinement order on
partitions.  Tau Core does not claim novelty for these facts.  It uses them as
bookkeeping discipline so that later physical branches cannot hide endpoint
leakage or double counting behind new terminology.  The same conceptual
territory overlaps with coarse-graining, marginalization, consistent-history
and decoherence-inspired languages, and Jaynes-style information-theoretic
statistical mechanics \cite{Griffiths1984,GellMannHartle1990,Jaynes1957a,Jaynes1957b}.

Let \(\RTspace\) be a parent-response space and let \(U_i\) be a
readout.  The first mathematical object induced by a readout is its
indistinguishability relation:
\begin{equation}
  r \sim_i r'
  \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
  U_i(r)=U_i(r').
  \label{eq:readout-equivalence}
\end{equation}
This relation is readout-relative.  A distinction can be invisible to one
sector and visible to another.  In this sense, a readout is not just a map to
observables; it is also a rule for what counts as the same state for that
sector.

\begin{definition}[Readout kernel]
The readout kernel of \(U_i\) is the equivalence relation \(\sim_i\) defined
by the readout-equivalence relation \eqref{eq:readout-equivalence}.  If
\(\RTspace\) has a linear
structure and \(U_i\) is linear, this reduces to the usual null space
\(\Ker U_i\).  In general it is an equivalence relation, not necessarily a
linear subspace.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Readout refinement]
A readout \(U_j\) refines \(U_i\) if
\[
  r\sim_j r' \Longrightarrow r\sim_i r'.
\]
Equivalently, \(U_j\) distinguishes at least as much parent-response
information as \(U_i\).
\end{definition}

This gives a minimal partial order on readouts.  A more informative readout is
not automatically more physical.  It may be harder to stabilize, harder to
observe, or more vulnerable to noise.  But the refinement relation prevents
one from treating all readouts as interchangeable.

\begin{definition}[Joint readout]
Given readouts \(U_i\) and \(U_j\), the joint readout is
\[
  U_{ij}(r)=(U_i(r),U_j(r)).
\]
Its equivalence relation is the intersection:
\[
  \sim_{ij} \;=\; \sim_i \cap \sim_j.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Joint readouts refine their components]
The joint readout \(U_{ij}\) refines both \(U_i\) and \(U_j\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(r\sim_{ij}r'\), then \(U_i(r)=U_i(r')\) and \(U_j(r)=U_j(r')\).  Hence
\(r\sim_i r'\) and \(r\sim_j r'\).  Therefore the joint equivalence relation
is contained in each component equivalence relation.
\end{proof}

This trivial observation is one reason multi-readout tests are valuable, but
it should not be confused with statistical independence.  Two readouts may be
correlated sibling readouts of the same parent.  A minimal observation model is
\[
  R_i=O_i[P]+N_i,
\]
where \(P\) is a shared latent parent, \(O_i\) is the sector readout operator,
and \(N_i\) collects noise, selection, and pipeline effects.  In cosmology, for
example, galaxy clustering, weak lensing, gas tracers, and CMB lensing can all
respond to the same matter/potential parent while using different observational
operators.

Therefore raw agreement between readouts is not, by itself, a Tau Core signal.
The stronger question is whether a common residual remains after conditioning
on the appropriate null parent \(P_0\):
\[
  \Delta R_i=R_i-O_i[P_0].
\]
For a cosmological branch \(P_0\) might be a matched \(\Lambda\)CDM
matter/potential parent; for another branch it would be the relevant standard
baseline.  A Tau Core-compatible excess would be residual cross-readout
structure that survives the declared null model, covariance, and
source-overlap ledger.  Here this is simply called source-overlap discipline.
It is not a validation statistic and not an independence claim.
Without this discipline, a joint readout may simply double count the same
source coordinate.

    \arccos|\langle\psi_0|\psi_1\rangle|,
      & \rho_i=|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|,\\[2pt]
    \arccos\sqrt{F(\rho_0,\rho_1)},
      & \text{mixed states},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:quantum-record-angle}
\end{equation}
where
\[
  F(\rho,\sigma)=
  \left[\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sqrt\rho}\right]^2
\]
is the Uhlmann fidelity.  Uhlmann's minimum-lift identity~\cite{Uhlmann1976} applied to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:common-gram-minimum-lift} gives
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon_O^2
 =2\left(1-\sqrt{F(\rho_{O,0},\rho_{O,1})}\right)
 =4\sin^2\!\frac{D_Q(\rho_{O,0},\rho_{O,1})}{2}.
 \label{eq:gram-bures-identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 D_Q(\rho_{O,0},\rho_{O,1})
 =2\arcsin\frac{\epsilon_O}{2}
 \geq\epsilon_O,
 \qquad 0<\epsilon_O\leq\sqrt2.
  \label{eq:record-distance-threshold}
\end{equation}
The final inequality uses \(2\sin(x/2)\leq x\) on
\(0\leq x\leq\pi/2\).  It is therefore a theorem of the common-Gram
completion, not a separate distance assumption.  Positivity of \(K_M\) alone
would still be insufficient: the decisive input is the global quotient
minimum-lift identity, which already includes all purification shortcuts.

For self-adjoint unitary transport, the Fubini--Study/Bures path obeys
\begin{equation}
  D_Q(\rho(0),\rho(\delta t))
  \leq\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_0^{\delta t}\!\Delta H(t)\,dt,
  \qquad
  \Delta H(t)^2=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho H^2)-
  \operatorname{Tr}(\rho H)^2,
  \label{eq:state-distance-speed-bound}
\end{equation}
with mixed-state extensions expressed through the corresponding quantum Fisher
information \cite{MandelstamTamm1945,AnandanAharonov1990,Taddei2013}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:state-distance-speed-bound} is restricted here to the
declared self-adjoint unitary protocol.  Open or noisy evolution requires its
own quantum-Fisher-information path-speed bound and cannot inherit the same
\(\Delta H\) formula without an additional proof.
For constant \(\Delta H\), combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:record-distance-threshold}
and \eqref{eq:state-distance-speed-bound} gives the exact common-Gram bound
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \delta t_{\min,O}\geq
  \frac{2\hbar}{\Delta H}\arcsin\frac{\epsilon_O}{2}
  \geq\frac{\hbar\epsilon_O}{\Delta H}.}
  \label{eq:qor}
\end{equation}

The inverse-energy-spread form and the Uhlmann chord-to-angle identity are
standard, not new Tau laws.  The distinct Tau statement is narrower: the
source-frozen morphological Gram chord is physically identified with the
observer carrier's Uhlmann-amplitude distance.  At the same
\(\Delta H\), two independently reconstructed observer carriers with
different source-frozen \(\epsilon_O\) have different forbidden
single-record regions, provided the common-Gram carrier is physically
occupied.  The numerical prefactor is fixed by the angle convention in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:quantum-record-angle}--\eqref{eq:state-distance-speed-bound}.
An ordering of \emph{observed} onset thresholds follows
only if the bound is saturated, or if independently controlled efficiencies
establish the same slack above the bound.  No detector gain may be fitted to
enforce either conclusion.

For an empirical test, ``independent reconstruction'' therefore has a precise
meaning: the carrier class, the two record prototypes, the embedding
\(\iota_O\), and the pullback metric \(G_O\) must be fixed from source-side
morphology and calibration data that exclude the timing endpoint.  If these
objects cannot be operationalized independently, Eq.~\eqref{eq:qor} remains a
conditional mathematical discriminator rather than a test-ready numerical
prediction.

Here a \emph{single stable record} means that one predeclared protocol run
places a pointer variable in one of two disjoint decision regions with
single-shot classification error at most \(\eta_{\rm rec}\), and that the
assignment persists for a predeclared hold time \(\tau_{\rm hold}\).  No
averaging across repeated preparations, tomographic reconstruction, or
multi-shot parameter estimation is part of that record event.  Those methods
may calibrate the carrier or estimate sub-threshold changes, but they test a
different operational question.

\subsection{Finite source criterion and carrier decision}

 \label{eq:a8-faithful-source-state}

 P_*=\operatorname{supp}G_*^{\rm rec}.
 \label{eq:a8-occupied-support}
\end{equation}

 \langle y,G_*^{\rm rec}y\rangle
 =\left\|K_*^{-1/2}(R_{\rm rec}^{\rm dedup})^\dagger y\right\|^2,

\subsection{A finite source theorem for A8b}

\subsubsection{Algebraic sufficient condition}
\label{sec:a8-algebraic-sufficient-condition}

Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak A_{\rm rec}\subseteq\operatorname{End}(\mathcal H_{\rm rec})
 \label{eq:recovery-algebra}
\end{equation}
be the finite unital star algebra generated by the source-owned primitive
recovery relations of the stabilized body, and let
\(Z_\alpha\in Z(\mathfrak A_{\rm rec}'')\) be mutually orthogonal central
recovery projectors with \(\sum_\alpha Z_\alpha=P_{\rm rec}\).
When a chain realization is used below, its descended incidence \(N\) and
reverse incidence \(N^\dagger\) are included explicitly among these
generators.  The chain-degree projectors \(P_k\) are not thereby identified
with the central recovery projectors.  The latter label reducing recovery
components and are required to preserve the occupied grading,
\([Z_\alpha,P_k]=0\); centrality additionally gives
\([Z_\alpha,N]=[Z_\alpha,N^\dagger]=0\).

Central recovery support and faithful occupation do not make these projectors
reducing for an independently supplied Dirac operator.  On \(\mathbb C^2\),
keep the diagonal recovery algebra, its two rank-one central projectors, and
the faithful state \(I/2\) fixed.  The pair
\begin{equation}
 D_0=\begin{pmatrix}d_1&0\\0&d_2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D_a=\begin{pmatrix}d_1&a\\a&d_2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad a\ne0,
 \label{eq:recovery-dirac-counterpair}
\end{equation}
has identical recovery-algebra and occupation data, whereas only \(D_0\)
commutes with the central projectors.  Recovery cyclicity and central support
therefore do not imply a Dirac direct sum.

The remaining transfer from spectral blocks to amplitude distances is not a
consequence of affiliation.  We make its exact content explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Blockwise spectral-to-amplitude transfer]
\label{lem:blockwise-spectral-amplitude-transfer}
Let the retained record tangent and the horizontal amplitude carrier have
orthogonal decompositions
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm rec}=\bigoplus_\alpha E_\alpha,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_{\rm amp}=\bigoplus_\alpha\mathcal E_\alpha,

\section{Nontrivial representation condition}

The parent/readout notation is not allowed to count as a Tau Core result by
itself.  Without an extra restriction, any effective theory could be written in
the trivial form
\[
  \RTspace=\Obs,\qquad \Rmap(s)=O,\qquad U=\mathrm{id}_{\Obs}.
\]
Such a representation is formally possible but scientifically empty.

Foundation Paper I therefore treats the following as a nontriviality
requirement rather than as a theorem:
\begin{quote}
A promoted Tau Core branch must use a source-declared parent response and a
readout family that cannot be reduced, without loss of the declared null,
source, or known-limit structure, to the identity readout on the endpoint
observable.
\end{quote}
Equivalently, the parent structure must do at least one of the following before
the endpoint is inspected: impose a quotient-safe null policy, relate two or
more readouts through a shared source coordinate, recover a known physical
limit, or exclude a wrong-family/control construction.  Otherwise the branch is
only a relabeling of the endpoint theory.

Known-limit recovery is not sufficient by itself.  Many harmless rewritings of
an existing theory can reproduce an already-known limit.  Promotion therefore
also requires at least one endpoint-blind consequence that could have failed:
a frozen numerical value, a forbidden family, a null prediction, a source-side
relation, or a control exclusion declared before endpoint inspection.

\section{Nulls, quotients, and protected identity}

Every physical readout has null directions: changes in the parent response
that the readout treats as physically irrelevant.  Some nulls are ordinary
gauge redundancies.  Some are observational limitations.  Some are protected
identity relations: distinctions that should not be counted as physical
differences in a declared sector.

Let \(N_i\) denote the null relation for readout \(U_i\).  The quotient object
is written
\[
  [r]_i \in \RTspace/N_i.
\]
The readout is quotient-safe only if
\begin{equation}
  r N_i r' \Longrightarrow U_i(r)=U_i(r').
  \tag{QS}
  \label{eq:quotient-safe}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
  N_i\subseteq \sim_i,
  \qquad
  U_i=\bar U_i\circ q_i,
  \qquad
  q_i:\RTspace\to\RTspace/N_i,
\]
for an induced quotient readout \(\bar U_i:\RTspace/N_i\to\Obs_i\).
If the quotient-safety condition \eqref{eq:quotient-safe} fails, the readout
assigns different observables to parent states that it also declares physically
equivalent.  Such a readout is internally inconsistent.

The converse is not required.  A readout can identify more states than the
declared null relation identifies.  That is precisely readout loss.  The
important issue is whether the loss is allowed.  In empirical branches this is
handled by a source-freeze discipline: the readout-relevant source
coordinates must be declared before endpoint scoring.  In the foundation
branch this becomes a source-priority problem: the selected readout should not
erase protected source identity unless the erased distinction is explicitly
routed to the null quotient.

A later formulation of the same idea is the readout-basin stability principle.
For a declared 4D readout \(R_{4D}\), a stable object-class \(O\) may be
represented by the basin
\[
  \mathcal B_O=\{r\in\RTspace: R_{4D}(r)\simeq O\}.
\]
Parent-response variation inside \(\mathcal B_O\) preserves the observed
object-class, while a visible transition or decay corresponds to crossing into
another basin.  In Foundation Paper I this is only a definition scaffold for
object persistence under non-injective readout; it is not a proton-stability
proof, a Standard Model derivation, or a decay-rate formula.

\begin{definition}[Protected source identity]
A protected source identity is an equivalence relation \(Q_{\rm src}\) on
parent-side seeds or source representatives that the theory treats as
source-level identity.  A readout rule is source-faithful if it does not
collapse distinct \(Q_{\rm src}\)-classes into one selected source class
without declaring the collapse as protected null structure.
\end{definition}

This definition is not an extra empirical claim.  It is a consistency demand.
Without it, a model can hide arbitrary distinctions in the parent space and
recover them only after seeing an endpoint.  That would make the theory
unfalsifiable.

\section{Endpoint blindness as a scientific rule}

Endpoint blindness is the operational face of the foundation idea.  A readout
model is endpoint-blind when the source, kernel family, amplitude rule, and
null policy are fixed before the target endpoint residual is inspected.

This is not a philosophical preference.  It is the only way a broad readout
framework can avoid becoming curve fitting.  If a galaxy rotation residual, a
cosmological distance residual, or a quantum outcome is used to choose the
source class that then explains it, the model has leaked the answer into its
input.

The minimal endpoint-blind record contains:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(E\arabic*)]
  \item a source object or source proxy;
  \item a declared readout family \(U_i\);
  \item a null and quotient policy;
  \item a source-overlap decision for all terminal readouts used together;
  \item a freeze artifact or equivalent timestamped declaration;
  \item forbidden endpoint fields;
  \item controls and failure conditions.
\end{enumerate}

Foundation Paper I does not run such an endpoint test.  It states why later
branches need it.  The philosophical content of Tau Core becomes scientific
only when it produces source-frozen rules that can fail.

\section{How effective readout-variation appears}

Tau Core replaces primitive time evolution by readout-parametrized variation.
Let \(r=\Rmap(s)\) be atemporal at the parent-response level.  Suppose a
readout \(U_i\) produces a record that can be coordinatized by a parameter
\(t_i\):
\[
  U_i(r) = o_i(t_i).
\]
Then the derivative
\[
  \frac{d o_i}{dt_i}
\]
is meaningful inside the readout.  It need not correspond to a parent-time
derivative.  This is the minimal sense in which this paper uses the phrase
``effective dynamics'': a parameterized variation internal to a readout, not a
derived physical equation of motion.

The distinction can be made sharper by considering two readouts of the same
parent response.  One readout may admit a temporal parameter \(t_A\), while
another admits a static relational record:
\[
  U_A(r)=o_A(t_A), \qquad U_B(r)=C_B.
\]
There is no contradiction.  The first readout organizes the parent response as
a time-indexed sequence.  The second organizes it as a correlation or
constraint.  A conventional physical theory may then choose the first
description because it is useful, local, and predictive.  Tau Core's claim is
weaker: usefulness of a temporal parameter does not make time primitive.

This is also why observer time must be sector-typed.  A clock used in a
galaxy-rotation analysis, a lensing time-delay analysis, a cosmological
distance analysis, and a quantum measurement protocol are not automatically
the same parent object.  They may share a local proper-time limit, but that
limit must be derived or justified inside the declared branch.

\section{Toy model with effective readout-time}

The finite model above can be extended to show effective readout-time.  Let
\[
  r=(a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n,c)\in \mathbb R^{n+2}.
\]
Define a readout
\[
  U_{\rm seq}(r) = (a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n),
\]
and choose the index \(k\) as an effective readout-time coordinate.  The readout sees
a sequence \(a_k\).  A finite-difference law can be defined:
\[
  \Delta a_k = a_{k+1}-a_k.
\]
Nothing in the parent object has evolved.  The parent response already
contains the indexed structure.  The readout has supplied an ordering and
therefore an effective readout-variation.

Now define a second readout
\[
  U_{\rm sum}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^n a_k.
\]
This readout sees no time sequence, only an aggregate.  The hidden component
relative to \(U_{\rm sum}\) is the loss of ordered information.  The hidden
component relative to \(U_{\rm seq}\) includes \(c\).  Again, hiddenness is
readout-relative.

\section{Seven foundation postulates}

The postulates below are not proved in this paper.  They define the candidate
framework.  Later papers or theory notes may try to derive or weaken them.

\begin{postulate}[Tau substrate]
There exists a parent-level structure \(\T\) that is not, at the foundation
level, ordinary spacetime evolution.
\end{postulate}

\begin{postulate}[Seed--body response]
Physical configurations are represented at the parent level by endpoint-blind
responses to seeds, stabilized as a morphology-conditioned body:
\[
  s \in \Ssrc, \qquad r=\Rmap(s)\in \RTspace,
  \qquad r\longrightarrow\Mta.
\]
The generative direction is seed/base to body; the body is not constructed
from a terminal residual or observer readout.
\end{postulate}

\begin{postulate}[Endpoint blindness]
The response \(\Rmap(s)\), and any source object used to select a readout
kernel, must be specified without using the endpoint residual that the readout
is later asked to explain.
\end{postulate}

\begin{postulate}[Readout emergence]
Observed physical descriptions are sector readouts:
\[
  O_i = U_i^{\Mta}(\Rmap(s)).
\]
Time, spacetime, quantum access, gravity, and mass/source descriptions are
readout candidates rather than primitive categories.  The readout mediates
the complete physical description, not merely a discrepancy relative to a
chosen standard model.
\end{postulate}

\begin{postulate}[Effective readout variation]
Time evolution appears only when a readout admits a stable ordering or clock
coordinate.  A derivative such as
\[
  \frac{d}{dt} U_i(\Rmap(s))
\]
is an effective readout derivative, not a primitive parent-time derivative and
not, by itself, a physical law of motion.
\end{postulate}

\begin{postulate}[Hidden defect]
If a readout and its allowed relift fail to recover the parent-response
representative modulo the declared null relation, the obstruction
\(\omega_i(r)\neq 0\) is recorded as a hidden defect of that readout.  A
coordinate difference \(J_iU_i(r)-r\) is only a chart-level representative of
that obstruction.
\end{postulate}

\begin{postulate}[Tension as readout failure]
Some observational or theoretical tensions may be modeled as readout,
closure, or relift defects.  This is a research hypothesis.  It becomes
physical only after source-frozen rules, controls, known limits, and held-out
tests are supplied.
\end{postulate}

\section{A compact finite toy witness}

This deliberately nonphysical witness records only the quotient logic.  Let
\(\RTspace=\mathbb R^3\), \(r=(a,b,c)\), and
\[
 U_A(r)=(a,b),\quad U_B(r)=(a,c),\qquad
 J_A(x,y)=(x,y,0),\quad J_B(x,z)=(x,0,z).

In this linear chart,
\[
 h_A(r)=(0,0,-c),\qquad h_B(r)=(0,-b,0).
\]
Thus a coordinate hidden under one readout can be visible under another.  For
\(U_A\), the quotient-safe null relation fixes \(a,b\) and ignores \(c\).
Changing the readout afterwards to
\(U_A^{\rm bad}(a,b,c)=(a,b+\alpha c)\) while retaining that null relation is
inconsistent: equivalent representatives would yield different observables.
Likewise, a source rule \(c=f_{\rm src}(a,b;\theta)\) is admissible only when
\(f_{\rm src}\) and \(\theta\) are frozen before endpoint inspection.

The witness proves no dark-sector, quantum, or gravitational mechanism.  It
only shows that hiddenness is readout-relative and that null policies and
source rules cannot be changed to rescue an endpoint.

\section{Morphological body versus projected morphology}

The phrase ``morphology'' can be misleading if left unqualified.  In ordinary
astronomical use it often means visible shape or catalogue type.  In Tau Core,
the primary object is not the observed shape.  It is the Tau-side
morphological body \(\Mta\).

The morphological body is the structured source object that conditions
readouts.  A galaxy image, a rotation curve, a cosmic-web tensor, a lensing
map, or a quantum measurement context can be a projected handle on the body,
but not the whole body.  This distinction became necessary because a single 4D
appearance can underdetermine the readout-relevant source.  For example, a
present projected shape may not contain enough information about global
incidence/order, observer/path geometry, closure class, or null structure.

For Foundation Paper I, the minimal role of \(\Mta\) is:
\begin{equation}
  O_i = U_i^{\Mta}(\Rmap(s)).
  \label{eq:morph-conditioned}
\end{equation}
The superscript indicates that the readout map is selected or conditioned by
the morphological body.  Equivalently one may regard \(U_i\) as a map on
\(\mathcal M_\tau\times\RTspace\).  This does not say that \(\Mta\) has been
physically derived.  It says that readout selection is not source-free.

For a concrete branch, \(\Mta\) must also be admissible and frozen.  This means
that the projected handles used to select \(\Mta\), the allowed family of
readout kernels, and any source-overlap decisions between terminal readouts must be
declared before endpoint scoring.  If \(\Mta\) is changed after seeing the
endpoint residual, the branch is phenomenological-only unless an independent
source review reopens the selection.

This point also prevents double counting.  If two proposed correction terms
use the same source coordinate, they should not be added independently unless
a source-side split justifies the separation.  Otherwise the readout model is
only relabeling the same source information twice.

\section{Quantum readout}

The weak-spine statement is simply
\(q=U_q^{\Mta}(\Rmap(s))\), where \(q\) is an access-state or
probability-assignment record.  That notation alone does not imply Hilbert
space, interference, or the Born rule.  Existing reconstruction programs show
how much operational, convex, symmetry, and composition structure is normally
required \cite{Hardy2001,Chiribella2011,MasanesMueller2011,BarnumWilce2014}.

assumptions to trace form is a conditional representation result.  What is not
derived is the physical Tau-parent selection of those valuation assumptions.
This distinction is essential: information loss or finite readout capacity by
itself gives a coarse graining, not specifically complex quantum mechanics.

Quantum and gravity are coupled in the MVP because

kept classical at the semiclassical leaf, while its second variation defines a

claim a nonperturbative metric-quantization theorem, a unique tensor-product
structure, interacting ultraviolet QFT, or a complete measurement ontology.
It claims the narrower common-functional and compatible-limit results of
Sec.~\ref{sec:mvp}.

\section{Position relative to MOND and dark-matter programs}

Tau Core should not be framed as anti-MOND or anti-dark-matter.  A safer and
more accurate position is that it tries to supply a third-level language in
which MOND-like phenomenology and dark-matter-like signals may both be
effective readouts of deeper source structure.

For the MOND program, the attractive point is that Tau Core does not begin by
assuming that the standard cold-particle halo picture is the whole story.  It
allows the possibility that galaxy-scale acceleration regularities are
effective consequences of a deeper gravitational/readout structure.  The
limiting point, from a strict MOND perspective, is that Tau Core does not
promote MOND itself to final ontology.  It treats MOND-like laws, if they
appear, as possible intermediate effective rules:
\[
  \text{MOND-like regularity}
  \quad\leadsto\quad
  \text{effective readout limit of a deeper morphology}.
\]
This is not a MOND derivation in this paper.  It is a claim-boundary statement:
Tau Core may try to explain why a MOND-like scale or relation appears, but it
must still derive the scale, recover known limits, and pass controls.

For the dark-matter program, the caution is different.  Tau Core should not say
prematurely that dark matter is an illusion.  Lensing, CMB, BAO, cluster, and
structure-growth data provide strong evidence for extra gravitational
structure in the standard cosmological inference pipeline.  A Tau Core branch
would have to ask a more decomposed question:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  \text{dark-sector signal}
  \sim{}&
  \text{gravitating content}
  + \text{baryonic remnants} \\
  &+ \text{modified response}
  + \text{readout/path/morphology effects},
  \end{aligned}
\]
where the equality is schematic, not a model equation.  The scientific task is
to determine which components are present in which terminal readout, and which
component, if any, behaves like collisionless matter.

Thus the intended public framing is:
\begin{quote}
Tau Core is neither anti-MOND nor anti-dark-matter.  It is a readout-level
formal language in which MOND-like acceleration laws, dark-matter-like
lensing/cosmological signals, and observer-dependent residuals can be
separated into source-frozen components and tested against one another.
\end{quote}
This framing avoids a victory narrative.  It does not say that existing
communities were simply wrong.  It says that different communities may have
identified different projections of a deeper problem, and that the useful
question is how to split those projections without endpoint leakage.

\section{Comparison with adjacent ideas}

Tau Core overlaps in spirit with several families of ideas, but it should not
be confused with them.

\paragraph{Relational time.}
Relational-time approaches show how dynamics can be recovered through
correlations.  Tau Core agrees that external time need not be primitive, but it
places this inside a broader readout atlas.  Time is one readout among several,
not the only reconstruction target.

\paragraph{Shape dynamics and relational configuration.}
Shape-dynamics-inspired work emphasizes that spacetime structure may be
reformulated through different symmetry or relational choices
\cite{GomesKoslowski2012}.  Tau Core uses this as a cautionary analogy: one
must state which structure is primitive and which is recovered.  It does not
import shape dynamics as a proof.

\paragraph{Quantum reconstruction.}
Quantum reconstruction programs provide a model for turning operational
principles into formal structure.  Tau Core's quantum branch must meet a
similar standard.  It cannot derive Hilbert space merely by naming a quantum
readout.  The adopted effect valuation in Sec.~\ref{sec:mvp-quantum} is an
explicit conditional completion of that gap, not a derivation from the weak
Tau spine.

\paragraph{Semiclassical and stochastic gravity.}
The coupled equation \eqref{eq:semiclassical-einstein} belongs to the standard
semiclassical-gravity family, and its metric second variation is naturally
related to stress fluctuations and stochastic gravity
\cite{HuVerdaguer2008}.  Tau Core does not claim those equations as new.  Its
candidate contribution is the body-first common ownership of gravity, quantum
effects, and observer records.

\paragraph{Locally covariant quantum field theory.}
Local covariance, microlocal/Hadamard regularity, and renormalized local
observables provide the appropriate standard for a QFT readout on curved
spacetime \cite{BrunettiFredenhagenVerch2003,HollandsWald2001,HollandsWald2002}.
The MVP assumes this infrared structure.  It does not reconstruct the full
local net or interacting theory from parent morphology.

\paragraph{Quantum speed limits.}
The QOR inequality has the same inverse-energy-spread dimension as
Mandelstam--Tamm and geometric quantum-speed-limit relations
\cite{MandelstamTamm1945,AnandanAharonov1990,Taddei2013}.  The proposed
discriminator is therefore not the inverse-energy bound or the standard
Gram-chord/Bures-angle conversion.  It is the source-frozen physical
identification of the morphological factor \(\epsilon_O\) with the occupied
observer carrier's Uhlmann-amplitude distance.

\paragraph{Holography and error correction.}
Holographic quantum error correction shows that bulk-like structure can be
encoded and recovered nontrivially from boundary data
\cite{AlmheiriDongHarlow2015}.  Tau Core treats this as an analogy for
readout/reconstruction discipline, not as evidence for the Tau ontology.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item deriving a non-circular parent response law \(\Rmap\);
  \item defining a source-realizable morphological body \(\Mta\);
  \item proving that a readout map has a quotient-safe null policy;
  \item deriving a known physical limit, such as proper time or Newtonian
  gravity, as a controlled readout limit;
  \item producing source-frozen empirical rules that survive held-out tests;
  \item preserving negative results by demoting failed kernels instead of
  retuning them after endpoint inspection.
\end{enumerate}

The framework has now passed the earlier milestone of constructing one worked
completion: Sec.~\ref{sec:mvp} fixes a variational response type, recovers two
standard leaves, and freezes one discriminator.  The next milestone is not
another general layer or another terminal.  It is to decide the physical
selection question in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mvp-nonselection}, preferably by deriving
the occupied completion from a deeper source law and independently by testing
Eq.~\eqref{eq:qor}.

The weakest useful form of progress is a toy model that exposes a blocker.  A
failed theorem can be valuable if it shows which extra principle is needed.
For example, local convexity or a positive Hessian may not force global
exposed-face duality; finite contextual grammars may not force Hilbert space;
and source stability may not force a unique source basis.  Such negative
results help keep the framework honest.

The weak Tau spine still permits several mathematical types for a parent
response law.  Candidate types include:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{variational law} & \Rmap \text{ selected by an action or extremal principle},\\
\text{constraint law} & \Rmap \text{ selected by admissibility or closure equations},\\
\text{algebraic closure} & \Rmap \text{ fixed by source/readout consistency},\\
\text{probabilistic kernel} & \Rmap \text{ given by an endpoint-blind transition kernel},\\
\text{fixed point} & \Rmap \text{ selected as a stable solution of a response operator},\\
\text{functorial rule} & \Rmap \text{ induced by structure-preserving source maps}.
\end{array}
\]
The MVP makes one finite choice---an ordered variational completion---without
claiming that all Tau branches must use it.  Any alternative branch must still
declare its type before claiming a physical law.

The largest remaining weakness is therefore not the absence of every worked
chain.  It is the missing implication from the physical source to the chosen
completion.  The relevant standard is now
\[
  \text{source}\to\Rmap\to U_i^{\Mta}
  \to \text{known limit or frozen falsifiable consequence}
\]
without endpoint leakage, together with a proof that the physical source
actually occupies the response law used in that chain.

\section{What would count as failure}

A reviewer should not have to infer the failure conditions.  At the foundation
level, Tau Core would be weakened or demoted if the following failure modes
persist after reasonable attempts to repair definitions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(F\arabic*)]
  \item \textbf{No source-realizable parent object.}  If no non-circular
  parent-side source class can be specified for a branch, the branch remains
  vocabulary rather than physics.
  \item \textbf{No quotient-safe readout.}  If declared null-equivalent parent
  representatives produce different observables, the readout is internally
  inconsistent.
  \item \textbf{No known-limit recovery.}  If a branch cannot recover the
  relevant standard limit, such as proper time, Newtonian gravity, Born
  probabilities, or \(\Lambda\)CDM behavior where appropriate, it should not
  be promoted.
  \item \textbf{Endpoint leakage.}  If the source class, kernel family, or
  amplitude rule is selected using the residual it later explains, the result
  is a fit, not a Tau Core derivation.
  \item \textbf{Uncontrolled rescue.}  If every failure is repaired by adding
  an unconstrained terminal after endpoint inspection, the framework
  loses scientific content.
\end{enumerate}

These are not merely methodological preferences.  They are the conditions
under which the present foundation language can fail to become a physical
research program.  A negative result satisfying these conditions should be
preserved as a real demotion, not hidden by changing terminology.

For later branches, demotion should be recorded explicitly.  The minimal status
labels have the following strict meanings:
\begin{center}

\toprule
Status & Strict meaning\\
\midrule
\textsc{toy-only} & no physical codomain, units, or operational observable supplied\\
\textsc{phenomenological-only} & fits or organizes known data but lacks parent-source derivation\\

\bottomrule

\end{center}
Forbidden rescue operations include post-hoc kernel exchange, endpoint-selected
morphological parameters, retrofitted path factors, and covariance or null
policy changes made after endpoint inspection.  A promoted branch must provide
at least one frozen numerical value, relation, exclusion, or null prediction
that could have failed.

\paragraph{Minimal failed-branch example.}
Let a toy branch declare a null policy \(n\sim n'\) and a readout
\[
  R(x,n)=x.
\]
If the branch later adds a correction \(A\,n\) to explain an endpoint residual,
then it has used a declared null coordinate as a physical observable.  The
branch fails quotient safety.  If \(A\) or the choice of \(n\)-dependence was
selected after inspecting the endpoint residual, it also fails endpoint
blindness.  Such a branch is not a Tau Core derivation; it is demoted to
\textsc{rejected} or, if no endpoint was used, at best
\textsc{phenomenological-only}.  This example is deliberately trivial: its
purpose is to show that the framework can say ``no'' before any exotic physics
is invoked.

\section{Likely objections}

A foundation framework should be written so that criticism has handles.  We
therefore list several likely objections and the intended answer.

\paragraph{Objection 1: this is only a change of language.}
This objection is serious.  The framework is only useful if readout language
forces new constraints.  The minimum non-linguistic constraints are endpoint
blindness, null/quotient safety, source-overlap discipline, known-limit
recovery, and explicit failure conditions.  Without these, Tau Core would be a
vocabulary rather than a research program.

\paragraph{Objection 2: the parent response is unobservable.}
The parent response is not directly observed by assumption.  But this alone is
not fatal; many theoretical objects are inferred through constrained effects.
The relevant question is whether a declared readout rule produces predictions
or exclusions that could have failed.  If no such rule is produced, the parent
response remains a metaphysical placeholder.

\paragraph{Objection 3: the framework can explain anything.}
This is the most important danger.  Tau Core must therefore preserve negative
results.  A failed frozen kernel should be demoted, not retuned after looking
at the residual.  A wrong readout family should remain wrong.  A source class
that cannot be independently justified should not be promoted.  If the
framework always rescues itself by adding another projection, it fails as
science.

\paragraph{Objection 4: atemporal response sounds like a block universe.}
The difference is the block-readout relation \eqref{eq:block-readout}.  A block
universe treats a 4D history as the final object.  Tau Core treats a 4D history as one possible
readout.  The distinction matters only if other readouts impose additional
source-frozen constraints.  If they do not, Tau Core collapses back into
ordinary effective description.

\paragraph{Objection 5: the displayed physical laws are assumed rather than
derived from the Tau parent.}
Partly correct.  Within the frozen completion, the terminal equations follow
from one variational packet and their compatibility is nontrivial.  But the
completion contains explicit Lorentzian, state/effect, and observer-carrier
assumptions, and the physical base--seed source has not been proved to select
them.  The manuscript therefore claims conditional recovery, not a
first-principles derivation of GR or quantum theory from the minimal Tau base.

\paragraph{Objection 6: the QOR inequality is only a quantum speed limit.}
The \(1/\Delta H\) dependence is indeed not new.  The only proposed new
content is the independently reconstructed same-source identity
\(x_A=x_Q\), equivalently the identification of the body ratio with the
Uhlmann chord.  The conversion from that chord to the Fubini--Study/Bures
record angle is standard and is proved inside the common-Gram completion, not
by positivity of the weak Tau spine alone.  The finite carrier prototype
closes the mathematical and uncertainty bookkeeping but does not yet supply a
physically occupied common-Gram carrier.  If \(\epsilon_O\) is fitted from the timing
endpoint, the prediction is circular.  A stable one-shot record below
the bound falsifies the completion; absence of a threshold ordering is
decisive only when bound saturation or equal slack has been established
independently.

\paragraph{Objection 7: the common functional may hide double counting.}
Equations~\eqref{eq:mvp-microscopic-ledger}--\eqref{eq:mvp-counterterm-relocation}
now supply the term-by-term effective ledger.  Retained body stress is varied
only from \(S_{\rm body}^{\rm ret}\); integrated quantum/apparatus stress and
effect responses come only from \(W_O^{\rm ren}\); local counterterms occupy
one side of the renormalization convention; and Green responses are inverse
second variations, not extra summands.  Mixed blocks are handled by the exact
Schur reduction \eqref{eq:mvp-schur-ledger}.  This closes the effective and
quadratic bookkeeping objection once microscopic ownership is supplied.  It
does not derive that ownership partition or a full nonlinear microscopic Tau
action.

\paragraph{Objection 8: fluctuating geometry may invalidate the Born leaf.}
Correct.  Equation~\eqref{eq:born-leaf} is established only on the fixed
descended metric under A6.  If effects or the carrier Hilbert structure vary
with unresolved body/metric fluctuations, positivity, completeness, and
noncontextual additivity must be proved on the joint algebra.  The present
paper supplies no such nonperturbative theorem.

\paragraph{Objection 9: semiclassical recovery may fail in extreme regimes.}
The displayed gravity leaf does not establish stability through black-hole
evaporation, Page-curve regimes, topology change, or large stress
fluctuations.  Such failures would delimit or reject this MVP completion; they
cannot be counted as evidence for a hidden Tau correction without a separate
source-frozen derivation.

\section{Structural predictions rather than anomaly corrections}

The ontological question is whether the Tau Core language can ever yield a
prediction that is not merely a correction to an anomaly in an existing
framework such as MOND, \(\Lambda\)CDM, GR, or quantum theory.  The answer in
Foundation Paper I is conditional.

A Tau Core prediction is not counted as structurally original merely because it
adds a residual term to a baseline equation.  It would count only if a
source-frozen parent/morphology rule implies a constraint before the target
endpoint is inspected.  The schematic form is
\[
  \mathrm{Struct}_{\tau}(s,\Mta)=0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mathrm{Constraint}(U_i^{\Mta},U_j^{\Mta},N_i,N_j),
\]
or an exclusion:
\[
  \mathrm{Struct}_{\tau}(s,\Mta)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \text{readout pattern }P\text{ is inadmissible}.
\]
Examples of the relevant kind of constraint include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a compatibility condition forcing two readouts to co-vary;
  \item a null/quotient rule excluding an apparent correction as double
  counting;
  \item a basin-transition invariant restricting which readout transitions can
  appear as stable 4D events;
  \item a rank, connectivity, or frame-gluing condition that must hold before a
  Hilbert-like or metric-like representation is allowed;
  \item a frozen source rule that predicts a missing, forbidden, or correlated
  feature in a holdout readout.
\end{itemize}

Such a prediction is Tau Core-specific only if it is fixed from the parent
source, morphological body, null policy, and readout compatibility rules
before endpoint inspection.  If the same structure is chosen because a known
baseline leaves a residual, it is an anomaly-motivated correction rather than
a parent-morphological prediction.  Equation~\eqref{eq:qor} is the first
conditional example in this paper: its morphology-dependent factor is frozen
upstream, while its role in quantum-state record distance is derived from the
explicit common-Gram completion identity.  It is not yet a validated physical
example because common-Gram carrier occupation, laboratory realization, and the
empirical test remain open.

\paragraph{A concrete exclusion at framework level.}
The framework does forbid one nontrivial modeling move.  Suppose two proposed
sector corrections are written as
\[
  \Delta O_i=A_i\,q,\qquad \Delta O_j=A_j\,q,
\]
where \(q\) is the same source-side coordinate or proxy.  Unless the branch
declares a source-side split \(q=(q_i,q_j)\), an orthogonality/covariance rule,
or a shared-parent covariance model before endpoint inspection, the two terms
cannot be treated as independent evidence or added as independent corrections.
They are the same source degree of freedom read twice.  Thus Tau Core forbids
``same-source double counting'' as an admissible explanation.  This is not a
new physical prediction, but it is a structural exclusion that goes beyond
renaming a residual.

\section{Mixed-state distance and QFI conventions}
\label{app:bures-qfi}

To avoid the common squared-versus-root fidelity ambiguity, this manuscript
uses the \emph{squared} Uhlmann fidelity
\begin{equation}
  F(\rho,\sigma)
  =\left[\operatorname{Tr}
  \sqrt{\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sqrt\rho}\right]^2,
  \qquad 0\leq F\leq1.
  \label{eq:appendix-squared-fidelity}
\end{equation}
The root fidelity is
\(f(\rho,\sigma)=\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}\).  The Bures angle used in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:quantum-record-angle} is therefore
\begin{equation}
  D_B(\rho,\sigma)=\arccos f(\rho,\sigma)
  =\arccos\sqrt{F(\rho,\sigma)}.
  \label{eq:appendix-bures-angle}
\end{equation}
For pure states, \(F=|\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle|^2\), so
\(D_B=\arccos|\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle|\), the Fubini--Study angle.

For a differentiable one-parameter mixed-state path, the infinitesimal Bures
line element and quantum Fisher information \(\mathcal F_Q\) satisfy
\begin{equation}
  ds_B^2=\frac14\mathcal F_Q(t)\,dt^2.
  \label{eq:appendix-bures-qfi}
\end{equation}
For unitary evolution generated by a self-adjoint \(H\),
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal F_Q(t)\leq\frac{4\Delta H(t)^2}{\hbar^2},
  \qquad
  \frac{ds_B}{dt}\leq\frac{\Delta H(t)}{\hbar},
  \label{eq:appendix-unitary-qfi-bound}
\end{equation}
which integrates to Eq.~\eqref{eq:state-distance-speed-bound}
\cite{Taddei2013}.  A nonunitary channel must instead use its actual QFI path
speed; Eq.~\eqref{eq:appendix-unitary-qfi-bound} is not assumed for it.

The common-Gram completion fixes the notation
\[
 x_O={\mathcal A_X^{PC}\over\mathcal A_*}
 =\epsilon_O^2=2(1-\sqrt F),
 \qquad
 D_Q=2\arcsin(\epsilon_O/2).
\]
Hence the linear inequality
\(D_Q\geq\epsilon_O\) is a conservative corollary, with equality only in the
zero-separation limit.  The exact state-distance quantum speed limit is the
first inequality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:qor}.
